2026-10-29 · 罡曜第四款原創棋 · 回到遊戲

  • 開局是和棋，不論誰先走。電腦把所有 4,857,600 個局面都算過：從這個開局，雙方都下最好時，沒有人能強迫取勝，薄荷先走或珊瑚先走都一樣。先走的一方有 5 種第一手，2 種保持均勢；另外 3 種會輸，但要再走 35 到 63 手才分出勝負。
  • 前幾版不夠好，所以改了。走到對面整排任何一格就贏的時候，遊戲只是在比誰快：和棋只有 1.3%，也找不到公平的開局。改成只有對面邊的正中格算贏以後，防守才有意義。動不了的一方原本可以跳過，但那樣每個公平的開局，都有一方在模擬中多贏 9 個百分點以上；改成動不了就輸，差距消失了。
  • 雙方程度相近時，勝負接近一半一半。模擬中雙方每步有 10% 機會隨機走錯：薄荷先走時，先手勝 49.5%、後手勝 50.5%；珊瑚先走時，50.9% 對 49.1%。
  • 值得學。只會看一步的玩家對上完美下法全部輸掉，平均撐 14 到 16 手。

1. 怎麼檢查的

棋盤有 25 格，四座塔的擺法有 25 × 24 × 23 × 22 = 303,600 種。再乘上太陽的 8 個位置和輪到誰，共 4,857,600 個局面。電腦從塔已經抵達目標、或一方無路可走的局面往回推，一層一層標出每個局面在雙方都下最好時的結果：輪到的一方會贏、會輸，或沒有人能強迫取勝（和棋）。這種做法叫逆推分析（retrograde analysis）。4 核心約 5 秒完成。

交叉檢查

  1. 兩套程式數走法：網頁上的規則引擎（JavaScript）和求解器（C）分開撰寫，連計算影子的程式也各寫一份。從開局數到第 6 手的所有走法序列完全相同：薄荷先走時是 5、25、135、729、3,848、20,938；珊瑚先走時是 5、25、110、572、3,137、16,312。
  2. 逐一驗算：由 JavaScript 引擎在 3,000 盤隨機對局中走到的 154,306 個局面，自己產生每一步的後續局面，再檢查表裡的結果是否正好由後續局面推得出來（包括幾手之內分出勝負）。0 個不一致。

比例算的是輪到薄荷、還沒分出勝負的局面，太陽在任何位置。「必勝」包含下一手就能抵達目標的局面。

只有正中一格算贏，防守的一方才擋得住：擋住一格，比擋住一整排容易。太陽走 8 個位置時，影子會斜著落下，局面變化多，最長的強制勝利從 58 手變成 100 手以上。最後一條改動是「動不了就輸」：它讓「用影子把對方困住」成為一種贏法。我們沒有找到單一原因說明它為什麼能消除實戰差距，但數字很清楚：改了以後，最公平的開局在模擬中差距不到 1 個百分點。

「任何影子都會罩住塔」是罡曜原創棋的共同想法：你的一手棋同時影響雙方。你的高塔可以困住對方，也可能困住自己的另一座塔。

選最後一個，是因為它在這幾個開局裡第一手的容錯最多：兩邊都是 5 種第一手裡有 2 種能守住，其他幾個只有 1 種，新手第一步就容易走錯。採用的開局用 20,000 盤再模擬一次：薄荷先走時 49.5% 對 50.5%，珊瑚先走時 50.9% 對 49.1%，一盤平均約 36 手，誤差約正負 0.7 個百分點。

4. 看得深有沒有用

一個只看一步的玩家：能馬上抵達目標就走，不走會讓對方下一手獲勝的棋，其餘隨機。它對上完美下法，四種組合（誰執薄荷、誰先走）各 4,000 盤，全部輸掉，平均在 14 到 16 手被擊敗。代表勝負常常來自好幾步以外的計畫，例如算準太陽轉到哪裡時，對方的塔會被影子罩住。

5. 電腦對手

電腦用 alpha-beta 搜尋，搜尋量固定（不看時間），所以同一個局面永遠回同一手。下表用完整的結果表打分：隨機抽 300 個輪到電腦、而且還沒輸定的局面，看它的一手有沒有保住原本的結果（必勝仍是必勝，和棋至少還是和棋）。

難度保住結果每步時間（Node）
簡單89.7%不到 1 毫秒
普通92.7%不到 5 毫秒
困難99.0%約 0.4 秒，最多 0.9 秒

困難難度對上完美下法下了四盤（執薄荷或珊瑚、先走或後走）：兩盤以三次重複和棋，兩盤到 200 手仍然沒有分出勝負。它仍不是完美對手：每 100 步裡約有 1 步會讓原本的結果變差。

6. 限制

  • 太陽只往一個方向轉，所以兩邊的處境不是完全對稱。公平是靠「誰先走都是和棋」的求解結果，不是靠形狀。
  • 模擬裡的失誤是隨機走一步，和真人的失誤不同。「只看一步的玩家」也只是新手的粗略替身。日影對真人是否耐玩，仍要靠真人試玩。
  • 模擬沒有套用三次重複判和，改用 300 手上限。
  • 只解了 5 × 5，塔高只試了五種組合（1 和 1、1 和 2、1 和 3、2 和 2、2 和 3）。
  • 主題和桌遊《Photosynthesis》（2017）相似：太陽繞著棋盤轉，物體投下影子。在那款遊戲裡，影子擋的是植物的得分；在日影裡，影子讓塔不能動。
  • 「和棋」是指雙方都下最好時的結果，不代表實戰常常和棋。

Riying: how the rules were made, and how fair they are

2026-10-29 · GambitAura original game 04 · Back to the game

  • The opening is a draw, whoever moves first. A computer worked through all 4,857,600 positions: from this start, with best play on both sides, nobody can force a win, with Mint or Coral moving first. The first player has 5 first moves; 2 keep the balance, and the other 3 lose, though it takes another 35 to 63 moves.
  • The first rules fell short, so they changed. When any square of the far row won, the game was only a race: 1.3% draws, and no fair start at all. Once only the middle of the far edge wins, defence starts to matter. A side that could not move used to pass, but then every fair start gave one side 9 or more extra points in simulated games. Once a side that cannot move loses, the gap disappeared.
  • Between equal players, results are close to even. In simulated games where each move has a 10% chance of being random: with Mint first, the first player wins 49.5% and the second 50.5%; with Coral first, 50.9% against 49.1%.
  • It rewards thinking ahead. A player who looks only one move ahead lost every game against perfect play, lasting 14 to 16 moves.

1. How it was checked

The board has 25 squares, so the four towers can stand in 25 × 24 × 23 × 22 = 303,600 ways. Times the sun's 8 places and whose turn it is, there are 4,857,600 positions. Starting from positions where a tower has reached its target or a side cannot move, the computer works backwards, marking every position with its result under best play: the side to move wins, loses, or nobody can force a win (a draw). This is called retrograde analysis. It takes about 5 seconds on 4 cores.

Cross-checks

  1. Two programs count moves: the rules engine on the page (JavaScript) and the solver (C) were written separately, down to the code that works out the shade. They agree on every move sequence from the opening to depth 6: with Mint first 5, 25, 135, 729, 3,848, 20,938; with Coral first 5, 25, 110, 572, 3,137, 16,312.
  2. Every value re-derived: for 154,306 positions reached in 3,000 random games, the JavaScript engine generated each position's next positions itself, and the table's result had to follow exactly from theirs, including how many moves until the end. 0 mismatches.

2. How the rules were chosen

Each set of rules was solved in full. Besides how many positions are forced wins and how long they last, one more thing was checked: whether a fair start exists, meaning both sides' towers on their own back row, placed a half turn apart, and a draw whoever moves first. Each place of the sun counts as its own start.

Sun at 4 places; any square of the far row wins; a side that cannot move passes71.1%27.7%1.3%49 moves0Only a race
Sun at 4 places; only the middle of the far edge wins; a side that cannot move passes52.9%26.6%20.5%58 moves66In the fairest starts, no first move is wrong
Sun at 8 places; otherwise the same52.0%26.4%21.6%107 moves102Every fair start gives one side 9 or more extra points in simulated games
Sun at 8 places; only the other side's shade stops you; a side that cannot move loses55.4%27.9%16.8%149 moves126In the fairest starts, no first move is wrong; and your own shade never gets in your way
Chosen: sun at 8 places; any shade stops a tower; a side that cannot move loses57.3%30.4%12.3%143 moves60

Shares count positions with Mint to move that are not decided yet, with the sun at any place. "Wins" includes positions where a tower can reach its target at once.

With a single winning square the defender can hold: blocking one square is easier than blocking a whole row. With the sun at 8 places, shade also falls diagonally, positions vary more, and the longest forced win grows from 58 moves to over 100. The last change, losing when you cannot move, turns trapping the other side in shade into a way to win. We found no single reason why it removes the gap in practice, but the numbers are clear: after the change, the fairest starts come within 1 point in simulated games.

"Any shade stops a tower" follows an idea shared by GambitAura's original games: your move affects both sides. Your tall tower can trap the other side, and it can also trap your own other tower.

3. How the opening was chosen

Under the chosen rules there are 60 fair starts. Each was simulated over 8,000 quick games (every move has a 10% chance of being random). These are the closest to even:

b1, c1North1/4 · 1/4+0.1
a1, c1East1/3 · 1/5+0.4
a1, c1West1/5 · 1/3−0.5
a1, d1South2/5 · 2/5+0.6

Coral's towers stand a half turn away. The quick simulation is good to about plus or minus 1 point, so these starts are equally fair within that.

The last one was chosen because it forgives the most on move one: for both sides, 2 of the 5 first moves hold, while the others allow only 1, so a beginner could go wrong on the very first move. Simulated again over 20,000 games: with Mint first 49.5% against 50.5%, with Coral first 50.9% against 49.1%, about 36 moves a game, good to about plus or minus 0.7 points.

4. Does thinking ahead pay?

A player who looks one move ahead reaches its target when it can, avoids moves that let the other side win next move, and otherwise moves at random. Against perfect play, over 4,000 games in each of the four combinations (who plays Mint, who moves first), it lost every game, usually within 14 to 16 moves. Wins often come from plans several moves deep, such as working out where the sun will be when the other side's tower falls into shade.

5. The computer player

The computer uses alpha-beta search with a fixed amount of search (not a clock), so a position always gets the same reply. It was scored against the full table: in 300 random positions where the computer is to move and not already lost, did its move keep the result (a win stays a win, a draw stays at least a draw)?

LevelKeeps the resultTime per move (Node)
Easy89.7%under 1 ms
Normal92.7%under 5 ms
Hard99.0%about 0.4 s, at most 0.9 s

Hard played four games against perfect play (as Mint or Coral, moving first or second): two ended in a threefold-repetition draw, and two were still undecided after 200 moves. It is still not a perfect player: about 1 move in 100 makes its result worse.

6. Limits

  • The sun only goes round one way, so the two sides are not exact mirror images. Fairness comes from the solved result, a draw whoever moves first, not from the shape.
  • A simulated slip is a random move, unlike human mistakes, and the one-move player is only a rough stand-in for a beginner. Whether Riying holds up for people still needs play-testing.
  • The simulation does not apply the threefold-repetition draw; it stops at 300 moves instead.
  • Only 5 × 5 was solved, and only five pairs of tower heights were tried (1 and 1, 1 and 2, 1 and 3, 2 and 2, 2 and 3).
  • The theme is close to the board game Photosynthesis (2017): a sun goes round the board and things cast shade. There, shade stops plants from scoring; in Riying, shade stops a tower from moving.
  • "Draw" means the result under best play on both sides. It does not mean real games are often drawn.
